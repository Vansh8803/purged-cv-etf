What these controls actually change, and what the numbers mean

The scheme changes training, not the strategy. All three schemes score the same model on the same bars. What differs is which rows were allowed into training: the control allows everything outside the test fold, purging removes rows whose label window overlaps the test fold, and walk-forward only ever trains on the past.

Standard K-fold is a control and is wrong for this data. It is run deliberately, so that the effect of purging can be measured at all. It must never be read as an honest result. Its folds are contiguous blocks of time, which is what sklearn.KFold does by default; that matters, because shuffling the same folds raises the share of leaked training rows from about 0.3% to about 88%.

Why the curves barely move between schemes. That is the project's finding rather than a bug. With contiguous folds only the rows at four fold boundaries overlap a test fold, so there is very little leakage for purging to remove. Fold geometry, not purging, is what decides how much a careless backtest flatters itself at this label length.

Cost and execution are applied here in the browser. Only the position series are precomputed — one per scheme and embargo. Crossing all four controls would be 1,764 equity curves and hundreds of megabytes, so cost and lag are arithmetic on top instead.

“One bar late” is a one-bar delay, not a next-open fill. An honest open fill needs open-to-close returns threaded through the entry bar, which cannot be done as arithmetic on a position series. A one-bar delay is a real cost of being late, but it is not the same thing.

Read Sharpe against its standard error. At roughly 4,300 daily bars that error is about 0.24 whatever the strategy, because it is set by the sample size rather than by the strategy. Most differences you can produce with these sliders are smaller than it.

Exposure is not a footnote. A strategy invested a fifth of the time forfeits most of the market's drift whatever it picks, and Sharpe scales roughly with the square root of exposure. Comparing a low-exposure model with buy & hold on Sharpe alone is not like for like.
