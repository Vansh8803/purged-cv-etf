Purged Cross-Validation for ETF Backtests

ES 335 · Vansh Dhameliya (25110337) · Aksh Hareshkumar Patel (25110017)
Code, data and every dated decision: github.com/Vansh8803/purged-cv-etf

We train one model to predict short-term moves in five ETFs, then score that same model, on the same data, under three different cross-validation schemes — and report how far apart the three answers are. The model is the vehicle. The spread between those numbers is the result.

1The question, and what one row is

Does leakage-safe cross-validation change what a backtest reports, and by how much? Financial labels are not points in time; they are windows. A label that takes ten days to resolve overlaps the next nine rows, so two nearby rows partly share an outcome. Ordinary cross-validation assumes rows are independent, and when they are not, training rows can carry the answers to test rows. The standard fix is purging — dropping training rows whose label window overlaps the test fold — and the question is whether it matters in practice.

One row is one trading day for one ETF. It carries that day's open, high, low, close, adjusted close and volume; ten features computed from the previous days; a binary label; and t1, the date that label resolved. That last column is what purging is defined in terms of.

What we claim The answer depends almost entirely on how the folds are cut, not on whether purging is applied. With folds that are contiguous blocks of time, only a third of a percent of training rows overlap a test fold, so purging has almost nothing to remove and changes the score by under a thousandth of an AUC point. Shuffle the same folds and 88% of training rows leak. Purging drives both to exactly zero. It is cheap insurance whose value is set by fold geometry.

2The data

Source. Yahoo Finance via yfinance, downloaded once on 2026-10-16 and cached in the repository. Nothing in the pipeline touches the network again: Yahoo restates adjusted prices retroactively, so a re-download would silently change numbers computed last week. The cached filenames carry the download date for that reason.

Universe. SPY, QQQ, IWM, TLT, GLD — five ETFs, not individual stocks. That choice is about survivorship bias: a list of stocks silently excludes everything that went bankrupt, whereas an index fund's price history already contains those companies from when they were in the index. GLD launched in November 2004, which is why the range starts in 2005 — a binding constraint, not a round number.

Size and quality. 5,282 daily bars per ETF, 2005-01-03 to 2025-12-30, no missing values, all five on the same NYSE calendar. After the 200-day warm-up needed by the longest moving average, and after dropping rows whose label cannot resolve inside the data, 5,056 rows survive per ticker — of which 4,302 are outside the holdout.

Labelled rows per ETF. label 1 is the share of pre-holdout days on which the upper barrier was touched first.

The one quality issue that mattered. Dividend-paying funds have an adjusted close that differs from the raw close, and our label depends on intraday highs and lows. Mixing raw intraday prices with a volatility threshold derived from adjusted returns would let a scheduled dividend drop trigger a false stop-loss. We scale high and low by Adj Close / Close before computing barriers. GLD distributes nothing, so its factor is exactly 1.0 on every one of its 5,282 rows — which makes it a free unit test of the scaling path, and the test suite asserts it.

3The label, and why it is shaped this way

For each day t we set two price barriers and ask which is touched first within ten trading days. Let sigma_t be the standard deviation of daily log returns over the previous 20 days. The barriers are a log move off that day's close:

upper = close × exp(+2.5·sigma_t)    lower = close × exp(−2.5·sigma_t)

The label is 1 if the upper barrier is touched first, and 0 if the lower one is, or if neither is within ten days. We also record t1, the bar it resolved on.

Barriers scale with volatility so that a label means the same thing in a calm month as in a crisis; a fixed ±2% would be unreachable in one regime and noise in another. Log space keeps the two barriers exact mirrors — upper × lower = close² — so the label inherits no upward bias from the arithmetic.

A decision that nearly broke the project We first set the barriers at ±1 sigma. At that width the median label resolved in a single bar, 52% of labels resolved at t+1, and the ten-day limit bound on under 1% of rows. Label windows that short barely overlap, so purging would have had nothing to purge and the headline result would have been an artifact of the barrier width rather than a finding about cross-validation. Widening to ±2.5 sigma gives a mean window near six bars and a timeout rate around 27%. The cost is real and belongs in the limitations: effective sample size falls from about 1,575 to 662.

How much data we actually have

Overlapping labels are not independent observations, so the row count overstates the evidence. For each calendar day we count how many label windows span it; a label's uniqueness is the mean of 1/that across its own window. Multiply the average by the row count and you get the honest denominator.

Concurrency peaks at 11, the most windows that can cover one bar when each spans [t, t+10]. Each ETF gets its own model, so these are not added up.

4Preprocessing, and the leak that is easy to miss

Ten features, all computed from information available on or before day t and then shifted one bar, so a row dated t holds values computed up to t−1: log returns over 1, 5, 10 and 20 days; 20-day realised volatility; volatility of that volatility; distance from the 20-, 50- and 200-day moving averages; and a 20-day volume z-score.

Computing them over the whole dataset is not a leak. Every one is a deterministic rolling function of its own past, so a row's value does not change depending on which fold the row lands in. Nothing is estimated from the sample.

Preparing them over the whole dataset is a leak. A scaler learns each column's mean and standard deviation. Fit it once on everything and every fold's test rows have contributed to the numbers used to transform them — the model never saw those rows, but information from them arrived through the back door. So the scaler and imputer are fitted inside each fold, on training rows only. The test suite spikes a test-fold value to 10⁹ and asserts the fitted scale does not move.

What the model is forbidden to see, and why one exclusion matters The raw bars at t are withheld, obviously. Less obviously, so is sigma_t — the volatility that sets the barrier width. Handing it over would tell the model how far price must travel for the label to fire. The shifted version, realized_vol_20d, is the honest one. This exclusion turned out to matter more than we expected; see the floor below.

5The baseline and the models

All five predict the same binary target, so precision and AUC mean the same thing in every row of the comparison. Two of them are not fitted at all, and are scored through exactly the same code path as the learned ones — same folds, same test rows, same metric.

Buy and hold acts every day. Not a model: the reference every strategy has to beat, and the number precision must be read against. Moving-average crossover is the baseline from our proposal — long while the 20-day average sits above the 50-day, applied one bar late. A rule cannot leak or overfit, so any difference a learned model shows between schemes is the learning rather than the data. Logistic regression on the standardised features is the floor: if nothing beats it, complexity has not earned its place. Gradient-boosted trees capture interactions a linear model cannot, with capacity deliberately held down because roughly 660 effective observations is the binding constraint. Meta-labelling lets the crossover rule pick direction and trains a model only on the days the rule fires, to decide whether to take the signal — it can filter, never generate.

No neural network, and no hyperparameter search. The settings are fixed and chosen for the sample size rather than tuned on it, which keeps the trial count honest at five.

6How we evaluated it

Three schemes, all built on one shared overlap rule so they cannot diverge.

Purged 5-fold with an embargo — the primary number. Folds are contiguous blocks of time. A training row is dropped when its label window [t, t1] overlaps the test fold at all, and additionally when it starts within the embargo window just after the fold. Standard 5-fold — the control, run deliberately and wrong for this data, so that the effect of purging can be measured at all. Walk-forward, expanding — corroboration; only ever trains on the past. A holdout, 2023–2025, declared before any modelling and opened once at the end.

The embargo's usual justification is serial correlation, which is true but vague. The concrete one here: our features use 20-day rolling windows, so a row just after a test fold has features computed from bars inside it. That also gives the length a principled floor of at least 20 bars.

How much of each scheme's training data overlaps the fold it is scored on, counted pairwise against each test label window.

That table is the project in one place. A contiguous control leaks a third of a percent; the same scheme with shuffled folds leaks 88%; purging and walk-forward leak nothing. We chose the contiguous control deliberately, because sklearn.KFold does not shuffle by default and because it is the harder test for our own claim — the shuffled variant would have handed us a spectacular gap from a strawman.

Metrics, and the baselines they must be read against

Precision is primary, because acting wrongly costs money and skipping costs little. It is read against the positive rate — what acting every day scores — not against 0.5, and it travels with coverage, the share of days the model acted at all, because precision alone rewards abstention. AUC asks whether the model ranks days correctly. Sharpe is reported with Lo's standard error, because a Sharpe ratio is a t-statistic in disguise and on this much data its error bar is wide. Maximum drawdown and turnover are what the headline hides. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts for having tried five models: the luckiest of five skill-less attempts still looks good, so the question is whether we beat that.

The no-signal floor is 0.544, not 0.500 We generated synthetic series with a known planted signal and ran the whole pipeline on them. On a pure random walk with zero price predictability, the full feature set still scores about 0.544 AUC, while the return features alone score 0.503. The mechanism: sigma_t sets the barrier width → wide barriers are less likely to be touched → an untouched barrier times out → a timeout counts as label 0. So trailing volatility predicts the label with no directional skill whatsoever. Every AUC on this page is read against 0.544. Without this check we would have reported an AUC of 0.55 as a modest edge when it is the floor. Measuring the same null on real bars, by randomising the sign of each return, gives 0.529 — so the synthetic figure is conservative.
What a null result is allowed to mean The same synthetic machinery bounds our own sensitivity. Planting AR(1) predictability of increasing strength, the pipeline starts to detect it around a coefficient of 0.4. The real lag-1 autocorrelation in these ETFs runs from −0.107 (SPY) to −0.010 (GLD) — an order of magnitude smaller, and the wrong sign for momentum. So a flat result here means “no AR(1)-style edge above about 0.4”, not “no edge”. That is the sentence this check buys.

7Results

The gap, which is the headline

AUC by model and scheme, averaged over the five ETFs, under the purged scheme at the configured embargo. Read against the 0.544 floor.

Logistic is the best ranker and the only model clearly above the floor. The trees are barely above it; both handwritten rules are below it, meaning a model that knew nothing except trailing volatility would beat our baseline. That is worth saying plainly rather than hiding: the baseline is not a strawman we beat, it is a rule that does essentially nothing on this target.

And the gap itself — control minus purged — swept across every embargo from 0 to 20 bars:

Units are AUC × 1000, because in AUC itself every figure is zeroes. Paired fold for fold: the control and the purged scheme split the same rows the same way, so their test folds are identical and only the training sets differ.

Three things are true at once. The gap is measurable — pool enough folds and it clears two standard errors. It is negligible — the largest value anywhere is about a thousandth of an AUC point, against a single fold's standard error of about seventeen thousandths, so the effect is roughly forty times smaller than the noise on the measurement it is drawn from. And it is not sign-stable: its direction changes from ETF to ETF. “Significant” and “meaningful” are not the same word, and this is a clean demonstration of the difference.

From a classifier to a strategy

A prediction is not yet a strategy. A signal at the close of t opens a position on the next bar and holds it until the label resolves at t1 — the trade the label actually scores. Overlapping signals do not stack: position is capped at one unit, so leverage does not become a function of signal density.

Pre-holdout, out-of-fold under the purged scheme, no costs. matched is the Sharpe from holding the same number of bars chosen at random, which separates how well a model chose from how often it was invested.
The best classifier is the worst strategy Logistic has the highest AUC and the lowest Sharpe. Ranking ability and profitability are different questions. The fall decomposes into three parts and only the last is turnover: logistic is invested 22% of the time, and a long-or-flat strategy at that exposure forfeits most of the market's drift whatever it picks — Sharpe scales roughly with the square root of exposure, so its ceiling is already about 0.22 against buy-and-hold's 0.48. Then it selects slightly worse-than-random days. Then costs. A project that stopped at the AUC table would have reported logistic as the winner.
Sharpe against trading cost. The trees trade 18.9 times a year and give up a third of their Sharpe by 10 bps; buy-and-hold trades 0.1 times a year and gives up nothing.

Almost nothing here is distinguishable from nothing. The Sharpe standard error is 0.242 for every model, because at roughly 4,300 daily bars it is set by the sample size rather than by the strategy. Only buy-and-hold reaches t = 2, and that is the index having risen. No Deflated Sharpe Ratio comes near a convincing bar: the luckiest of five skill-less tries would be expected to show an annualised 0.25, and the best DSR on the page is 0.82.

8Error analysis

An error is a day the model said act and the lower barrier fell first, out-of-fold under the purged scheme — so every one is a day the model had genuinely not been trained on.

Count of errors by quintile of trailing volatility on the day.

Errors concentrate where the barriers are widest, and this is the most useful thing in the analysis. Error rate climbs from about 0.48 in the lowest volatility quintile to about 0.64 in the highest. That is the volatility channel from the floor showing its other face: high volatility means wide barriers, which means timeouts, which count as 0 — so the conditions in which the models are most often wrong are exactly the conditions their edge is built on. The edge and the failures have the same cause. By year, 2008 is the worst for every model.

The costliest individual errors, by realised close-to-close return over the trade. Confidence is the model's own probability.

They are almost all October–November 2008, January 2009 and March 2020, and mostly IWM, the small-cap fund with the widest swings. Confidence on these was ordinary, between about 0.52 and 0.70: the models were not unusually sure, they were ordinarily sure and catastrophically wrong. The honest sentence is that these models are least reliable exactly when it matters most, and that this follows from the label's construction rather than from a bug.

10The holdout, opened once

2023-01-01 to 2025-12-31, fixed in config.yaml on 2026-10-14 before any modelling, and untouched until everything above was finished. Each model was fitted a single time on the purged pre-holdout rows and predicted roughly 744 held-back bars. Nothing was tuned, selected or retried, and the numbers were written into the decision log before being discussed.

Holdout results, averaged over the five ETFs, no costs. Precision is read against the positive rate of 0.437.
The purged estimate predicted out-of-sample AUC almost exactly Logistic scored 0.5755 under the purged scheme before the holdout and 0.5754 on it — a difference of one ten-thousandth, on three years nobody had looked at. Every model held within 0.024. Whatever the pipeline was measuring, it measured it stably. That is the strongest evidence on this page that the evaluation machinery works, and it is what the project was built to establish.

Buy-and-hold still wins, and by more than before — its Sharpe rose from 0.480 to 1.116. That is the market, not skill: over the period SPY returned +88%, QQQ +138%, GLD +133%, IWM +49% and TLT −3%. Every strategy's Sharpe roughly doubled for the same reason, and no model beat the index, which is the same conclusion as before the holdout. Nothing reaches t = 2, because the Sharpe standard error on 744 bars is 0.580 rather than 0.242.

Our one significant finding reversed, and that is the lesson Before the holdout, logistic's exposure-matched selection edge was −0.164 at t = −2.36 — the only result in the project that cleared two standard errors — and we wrote that it was measurably picking bad days. On the holdout the same model's edge is +0.179. A quantity that reverses sign out of sample was noise, and the earlier significance was a false positive: exactly the kind this project exists to warn about. We found it in our own strongest claim, which is uncomfortable and is why it is reported here rather than quietly dropped.

The trees' holdout selection edge of +0.292 at t = 3.4 is the most impressive-looking number in the final table, and we decline it. It is one figure among roughly sixty, drawn from five ETFs with four degrees of freedom, and its pre-holdout value was +0.071 at t = 1.37 — it quadrupled, which is what noise does. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio exists to price precisely this and puts the trees at 0.860, short of any convincing bar. Reporting it as skill would be the error this whole project is about.

11Limitations

The effective sample is the binding constraint. 4,302 rows per ETF carry roughly 662 independent observations once label overlap is accounted for. Every error bar on this page follows from that, and most differences we could report are smaller than their own uncertainty.

The barrier width is our most consequential single choice. At ±1 sigma the project would have had nothing to measure; at ±3.16 it would have had a third fewer effective observations. We picked ±2.5 and reported the sensitivity, but the result is conditional on it.

The label merges two different outcomes. “Went down” and “went nowhere” are both label 0, and only the second is predictable from volatility. That is the source of the 0.544 floor and of the error concentration in high-volatility regimes. A three-class target would separate them, at the cost of comparability across the five models.

Cross-sectional overlap is unhandled. The five ETFs share calendar dates, so rows overlap across tickers as well as across time. We fit one model per ETF to keep purging strictly one-dimensional, which sidesteps the problem rather than solving it.

Four of five folds train partly on the future. The cross-validated equity curves are a measure of generalisation, not a simulation of trading. Walk-forward is the scheme that does not do this, and it agrees.

Lo's standard error assumes IID returns. Daily returns are serially correlated and volatility clusters, so 0.242 understates the true error. It is still better than quoting none.

The 200-day moving average cannot be embargoed away. No embargo we could afford covers a 200-day lookback, so a small amount of feature overlap survives at every fold boundary.

The deflation's trial distribution is approximate. It uses the spread of Sharpes across five structurally different strategies, two of which are fixed references rather than search outcomes, where the formula assumes draws from a zero-skill distribution. The approximation is conservative — it widens the benchmark and lowers every DSR.

The holdout is short. 744 bars gives a Sharpe standard error of 0.580, so it can confirm that the AUC estimate held but it cannot resolve any difference between strategies.
